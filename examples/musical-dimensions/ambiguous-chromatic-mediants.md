# Ambiguous chromatic-mediant progression

## Reusable progression

- **ID:** `progression-ambiguous-c-to-ab-to-e-to-bb`
- **Intent:** create dramatic colour through major chords related by non-diatonic root motion
- **Tonal-centre behaviour:** ambiguous
- **Roman-numeral view from C:** `I | ♭VI | III | ♭VII`
- **Concrete reference chords:** C | A♭ | E | B♭
- **Bars:** one chord per bar

The Roman numerals are descriptive coordinates, not a claim that the progression follows conventional functional harmony in C.

## Why it works musically

Useful explanations include:

- chromatic-mediant relationships between C, A♭, and E
- common-tone retention between selected voicings
- semitone voice leading between chord tones
- strong root contrast with repeated major quality
- a possible C-centred opening and B♭-to-C return without a conventional dominant cadence

Do not assign tonic, predominant, and dominant labels unless a specific arrangement makes those functions audible.

## Reference voicing strategy

Choose voicings that minimize unnecessary motion:

- retain common tones when available
- move one or two upper voices by semitone or whole tone
- keep the bass roots explicit so the harmonic colour remains clear
- avoid default open-position shapes if they obscure the voice-leading purpose

## Practice session

- **Primary goal:** make an unusual progression sound intentional through voice leading and dynamics
- **Duration:** 20 minutes
- **Tempo:** 68 BPM
- **Meter:** 4/4
- **Technique:** clean arpeggiation or volume-swollen chord entries
- **Fretboard view:** triad sets on the top three or four strings
- **Ear target:** identify retained and moving tones before playing
- **Genre layers:** atmospheric rock, alt-rock, cinematic

### Warmup

1. Play C, A♭, E, and B♭ triads in one neck region.
2. Name the nearest common or semitone-related tones between each pair.
3. Sing the highest voice through the sequence.

**Readiness cue:** the top voice forms a deliberate line rather than jumping with each shape.

## Phrasing, dynamics, and space

- Enter each chord after beat one on the first pass.
- Let each chord decay before the next entry.
- Build from quiet C to strongest E, then release intensity on B♭.
- On the second pass, add one two-note melodic answer only after bars 2 and 4.
- Reserve the lower-mid register for bass or keyboard support.

## Evidence

Record one sparse pass and one denser pass.

Review:

- Did the progression sound intentional without a conventional cadence?
- Which voice-leading choice created the strongest continuity?
- Did the delayed entries and decay clarify the colour?
- Did added notes improve the progression or merely fill space?
- What was the largest audible defect?

## Variants

- Replace B♭ with G for a stronger return toward C.
- Hold a G pedal tone above all four chords.
- Move the progression to another reference centre while preserving interval relationships.
- Change one major chord to minor and record whether the identity materially changes.
