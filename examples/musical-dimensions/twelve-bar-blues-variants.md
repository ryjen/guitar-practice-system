# Twelve-bar blues variants

## Family

- **Parent ID:** `progression-blues-twelve-bar`
- **Intent:** compare how small harmonic changes alter momentum, tension, and genre vocabulary
- **Default form:** 12 bars
- **Default functions:** tonic, subdominant, dominant

## Standard dominant blues

- **ID:** `progression-blues-twelve-bar-dominant`
- **Roman numerals:** `I7 | I7 | I7 | I7 | IV7 | IV7 | I7 | I7 | V7 | IV7 | I7 | V7`
- **Reference key:** A
- **Chords:** A7 | A7 | A7 | A7 | D7 | D7 | A7 | A7 | E7 | D7 | A7 | E7

## Quick-change blues

- **ID:** `progression-blues-twelve-bar-quick-change`
- **Relationship:** variant of `progression-blues-twelve-bar-dominant`
- **Change:** bar 2 moves to IV7 before returning to I7
- **Roman numerals:** `I7 | IV7 | I7 | I7 | IV7 | IV7 | I7 | I7 | V7 | IV7 | I7 | V7`

The quick change creates earlier motion without changing the overall form.

## Minor blues

- **ID:** `progression-blues-twelve-bar-minor`
- **Roman numerals:** `i7 | i7 | i7 | i7 | iv7 | iv7 | i7 | i7 | ♭VI7 | V7 | i7 | V7`
- **Reference key:** A minor
- **Chords:** Am7 | Am7 | Am7 | Am7 | Dm7 | Dm7 | Am7 | Am7 | F7 | E7 | Am7 | E7

This is one practical minor-blues realization, not the only canonical form.

## Jazz-blues extension

- **ID:** `progression-blues-twelve-bar-jazz-basic`
- **Roman numerals:** `I7 | IV7 | I7 | vi7 II7 | IV7 | ♯iv°7 | I7 vi7 | ii7 V7 | iii7 VI7 | ii7 V7 | I7 VI7 | ii7 V7`
- **Purpose:** introduce turnarounds, secondary dominants, diminished passing motion, and denser harmonic rhythm

Do not treat the jazz form as a replacement for the simpler blues identities. It is a derived vocabulary layer.

## Comparative session

- **Primary goal:** hear form and variant identity without losing groove
- **Duration:** 25 minutes
- **Reference key:** A
- **Tempo:** 82 BPM
- **Meter:** 4/4
- **Feel:** shuffle
- **Technique:** rhythm comping plus two-bar lead responses
- **Genre layers:** blues; optionally blues-rock, country-blues, or jazz

### Passes

1. Standard dominant form with one voicing family.
2. Quick-change form with the same rhythm and voicings.
3. Minor form with the same density.
4. Jazz-blues form at a slower tempo if needed.

## Phrasing, dynamics, and space

- Comp for two bars, then leave one beat open before a short response.
- Maximum six lead notes per response.
- Use a quieter dynamic floor on bars 1–4 and build toward bars 9–10.
- Let the final tonic chord decay before the turnaround.
- On one pass, leave bar 12 empty except for the rhythm section to hear the return to bar 1.

## Ear targets

- Identify bars 5, 9, and 11 without counting from the beginning.
- Sing roots through one chorus.
- Hear whether bar 2 remains tonic or changes to subdominant.
- Distinguish the minor-blues ♭VI7–V7 motion from the dominant-blues V7–IV7 motion.

## Evidence

Record two adjacent variants without stopping.

Review:

- Was the 12-bar form still clear?
- Did the variant change sound intentional rather than mistaken?
- Did dynamics lead toward bars 9–10?
- Did the response gaps improve the groove?
- What should remain fixed for the next comparison?
