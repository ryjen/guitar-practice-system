# CAGED with interval and contextual ear training

## Session intent

Use one CAGED region as a temporary visual frame for hearing chord tones and connecting adjacent positions. CAGED is a navigation aid here, not the canonical fretboard model.

- **Session ID:** `session-caged-interval-ear-a-major`
- **Primary goal:** locate and hear scale degrees around an A-major chord without defaulting to a memorized scale run
- **Duration:** 25 minutes
- **Tonal centre:** A major
- **Progression:** A | D | E | A
- **Tempo:** 72 BPM
- **Meter:** 4/4
- **Fretboard region:** E-shape A major around the 5th fret, then one adjacent region
- **Technique focus:** clean fretting, position shifts, chord-tone targeting

## Reusable dimensions

- **Fretboard systems:** CAGED plus intervallic view
- **Intervals:** root, major third, perfect fifth, major sixth, major seventh
- **Ear targets:** sing before playing; distinguish chord tone from colour tone; hear resolution into the next chord
- **Expression:** short motifs, dynamic contour, deliberate releases
- **Space:** one full beat after each answer phrase

## Goal-directed warmup

1. Play the E-shape A-major chord and name every root.
2. Find the nearest major third and perfect fifth above and below each root.
3. Sing root–third–fifth before playing it.
4. Play the same three tones in a second nearby position without running the full scale.

**Readiness cue:** each target tone can be found from the chord shape and identified by sound before it is played.

## Stage 1 — chord-shape orientation

- Hold the E-shape chord or partial triad.
- Play only roots for one pass.
- Add thirds on the next pass.
- Add fifths on the next pass.
- Avoid filling intervening notes merely because they are under the fingers.

**Exit gate:** roots, thirds, and fifths can be located in random order without tracing a scale box.

## Stage 2 — contextual intervals

Over `A | D | E | A`:

- Target the third of each chord on the first pass.
- Target the fifth on the second pass.
- Use the major sixth over A as a colour tone, then resolve it to the fifth or root.
- Use G♯, the major seventh of A, as directed tension into the final A chord rather than as a resting tone.

Before each pass:

1. Sing the target degree.
2. Play it against the chord.
3. State whether it sounded stable, colourful, or unresolved.

## Stage 3 — adjacent-position connection

- Choose one adjacent CAGED region.
- Connect the two regions using a single shared chord tone.
- Shift positions only after a phrase ends.
- Keep the same motif through both positions so the neck movement does not replace musical continuity.

## Phrasing, dynamics, and space

- Use two- or three-note motifs.
- Maximum six notes per phrase.
- Leave beat four empty after each phrase.
- First phrase: quiet statement.
- Second phrase: same contour with stronger attack.
- Third phrase: change only the ending interval.
- Let the final note decay before changing position.

The goal is to hear the interval and phrase identity, not to prove complete fretboard coverage.

## Evidence task

Record:

1. one pass targeting thirds
2. one pass targeting fifths
3. one pass connecting two positions through a shared tone

Review:

- Could each interval be sung before it was played?
- Did the chord shape help orientation without trapping the phrase inside one box?
- Did the position shift preserve the motif?
- Did the empty beat make the target tone easier to hear?
- What was the largest audible defect?

## Fallback version

For a 10-minute session:

1. stay in one E-shape region
2. target only root, third, and fifth
3. record four short phrases with one beat of silence after each
4. review pitch recognition and phrase clarity only

## Variants

- Repeat with 3NPS or a horizontal single-string view.
- Use A Mixolydian and compare the minor seventh against the major seventh.
- Restrict the session to triads on the top three strings.
- Reverse the process: hear a target interval first, then choose the nearest fretboard representation.
