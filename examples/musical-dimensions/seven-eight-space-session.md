# 7/8 phrasing with explicit space

## Reusable rhythm

- **ID:** `rhythm-seven-eight-223`
- **Meter:** 7/8
- **Grouping:** `2+2+3`
- **Primary pulse cue:** `ONE-two ONE-two ONE-two-three`
- **Intent:** feel one asymmetrical cycle rather than seven isolated eighth notes
- **Default accent pattern:** accents on eighth-note positions 1, 3, and 5

The reusable rhythm does not own tempo, note material, key, or phrasing density.

## Session realization

- **Primary goal:** create clear call-and-response phrases without losing the 2+2+3 pulse
- **Duration:** 25 minutes
- **Tempo:** 72 BPM, dotted-quarter pulse where useful
- **Tonal centre:** A
- **Mode:** Dorian
- **Progression:** Am7 | D7, one bar each
- **Technique:** bend intonation, controlled vibrato, muted attacks
- **Fretboard view:** fifth-position region; CAGED naming optional
- **Ear targets:** minor third, fifth, and natural sixth
- **Genre layers:** blues plus atmospheric rock

## Warmup

1. Clap the 2+2+3 accents while counting aloud.
2. Play one muted note on each group accent.
3. Sustain A across a full bar and release exactly before the next downbeat.
4. Play the natural sixth, F♯, against Am7 and sing it before repeating.

**Readiness cue:** the grouping remains stable when the guitar stops playing.

## Phrase constraints

### Call

- Begin after the first two-note group.
- Maximum five notes.
- End before the final three-note group completes.

### Response

- Leave at least one complete two-note group silent.
- Begin on the final three-note group or wait until the next bar.
- Change only one aspect of the call: ending, contour, dynamic, articulation, or target tone.

### Density

- Maximum eight notes across each two-bar call-and-response pair.
- Every fourth two-bar pair is fully silent except for the backing track.
- Do not add pickups during the silent pair.

## Dynamics and articulation

- First pair: quiet, mostly legato.
- Second pair: stronger attack with one accented target note.
- Third pair: crescendo across the call, decrescendo across the response.
- Fourth pair: silence.

Use vibrato only after pitch settles. Let sustained notes decay naturally instead of filling the remaining subdivision.

## Backing-track room

- Bass states roots only on group accents.
- Drums emphasize 2+2+3 without constant fills.
- Pad or keyboard stays above the guitar’s main register.
- No rhythmic comping during the designated response window.

## Evidence task

Record four two-bar pairs without a metronome click in the final take; use the backing groove only.

Review:

- Did the 2+2+3 pulse remain audible during rests?
- Were phrase entries deliberate rather than late?
- Did the silent fourth pair strengthen the return?
- Did sustained decay preserve continuity?
- Did the response vary one idea or introduce unrelated material?
- What was the largest audible defect?

## Fallback version

For a 10-minute or fatigue-limited session:

1. Clap and mute the grouping for two minutes.
2. Play call-and-response using only A, C, E, and F♯.
3. Record two pairs, with the second pair leaving the response bar empty.
4. Review only pulse stability and quality of space.

## Variants

- Change grouping to `3+2+2` while preserving total meter.
- Keep `2+2+3` but displace the call entry.
- Use 7/4 to feel the same grouping at a larger scale.
- Replace the Dorian vamp with a single tonal-centre drone.
