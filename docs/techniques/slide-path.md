# Slide Guitar Path

## Goal

Develop reliable, expressive slide playing without requiring a dedicated guitar. The initial path uses standard tuning and a normal setup; open tunings are an optional branch after intonation, muting, and phrasing are stable.

## Default setup

- Use a slide that stays secure without squeezing the finger.
- Start on the ring or little finger; choose based on muting control and ability to fret normally.
- Keep the existing guitar setup unless fret contact makes clean slide notes impractical.
- Use clean or edge-of-breakup gain with limited delay/reverb while diagnosing pitch and noise.
- Prefer a tuner, keyboard, fretted reference note, or sustained backing harmony for pitch comparison.

### Setup trade-offs

| Choice | Benefit | Cost | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| Little-finger slide | Leaves three fingers available for fretting | Often less stable | Try first when mixed fretting/slide matters |
| Ring-finger slide | More control and mass | Reduces fretting options | Prefer when stability is the larger problem |
| Higher action | More tolerance before fret contact | Changes ordinary fretting feel | Defer until the current setup is proven limiting |
| Heavier strings | More resistance and sustain | Harder bends and normal playing | Optional, not required |
| Standard tuning | Existing fretboard knowledge; mixed playing | Fewer idiomatic full chords | Primary path |
| Open G / open E | Easy major chords and resonant vocabulary | New map; setup and tension implications | Later branch |

## Baseline recording

Record one dry or minimally processed take containing:

1. Ten sustained reference-note matches across at least three strings.
2. Four non-adjacent string changes.
3. A short melody of four to eight bars.
4. One uninterrupted chorus over `slide-slow-blues-a-60`.

For each section, note the largest audible problem: pitch, attack, release, adjacent-string noise, behind-the-slide noise, vibrato, timing, or tension.

The recording itself remains local or externally linked. Add only its stable identifier and observations to the technique evidence table.

## Progression

### Phase 1 — contact and pitch centre

Focus:

- Minimum slide pressure
- Position directly over the fret
- Stable sustained notes without vibrato
- Hearing sharp versus flat approaches

Practice:

- Match a fretted note with the slide.
- Approach each target from below, then above.
- Hold four beats and release cleanly.

Gate:

- Eight of ten notes settle recognizably on pitch within one beat.
- No fret rattle or dominant sympathetic ringing.

### Phase 2 — two-hand muting

Focus:

- Fingers behind the slide remain in light contact.
- Picking-hand thumb and fingers own unused strings.
- Note endings are intentional.

Practice:

- Isolated quarter notes on one string.
- Alternate strings 2 and 4, then 1 and 3, then 3 and 5.
- Record without delay or heavy reverb.

Gate:

- Three consecutive passes have clean attacks and releases.
- Unused strings never become the loudest sustained sound.

### Phase 3 — movement and vibrato

Focus:

- Time the travel, not only the destination.
- Settle before adding vibrato.
- Vibrato oscillates around the pitch centre and can stop on pitch.

Practice:

- Two-fret and five-fret shifts over one or two beats.
- Hold two beats, add vibrato for two beats, stop on centre.
- Repeat with narrow/slow and wider/faster deliberate variants.

Gate:

- Arrival matches the pulse.
- Vibrato onset, width, and stop are intentional in three consecutive passes.

### Phase 4 — melody and string changes

Focus:

- Preserve rhythm and contour before decoration.
- Cross adjacent strings without uncontrolled transients.
- Use slides, attacks, and silence as phrasing choices.

Practice:

- Sing or fret a short melody, then reproduce it with slide.
- Limit early melodies to two strings and a small register.
- Add one intentional scoop, one delayed vibrato, and one clean unornamented note.

Gate:

- Melody remains identifiable without accompaniment.
- Pitch errors do not obscure phrase shape.
- String changes are quieter than the intended notes.

### Phase 5 — harmony-aware phrasing

Focus:

- Target chord tones rather than moving a fixed lick over every chord.
- Use root, third, fifth, and flat seventh as audible landmarks.
- Leave space for the rhythm section.

Practice:

- Over the slow blues track, restrict each chorus to two target tones per chord.
- Use one chorus with no more than four notes per bar.
- Use call-and-response between registers.

Gate:

- Important arrivals agree with the underlying harmony.
- Phrases have clear starts, endings, and space.
- A complete uninterrupted chorus remains in control.

### Phase 6 — mixed fretting and slide

Focus:

- Use fretted notes, open strings, and slide as complementary articulations.
- Preserve muting while changing hand posture.
- Avoid treating every note as a glissando.

Practice:

- Alternate a fretted phrase and slide response.
- Fret behind or beside the slide only where the chosen finger permits it safely.
- Use open strings as drones only when harmonically intentional.

Gate:

- Switching techniques does not create a large noise spike or timing break.
- The articulation choice is musically audible and repeatable.

### Phase 7 — optional open-tuning branch

Enter this phase only after the standard-tuning musical-context gate is reliable.

Recommended order:

1. Open G for lower-tension roots/rock vocabulary and familiar major-triad layout.
2. Open E or open D for broader interval layout and sustained major harmony.

Open E raises string tension relative to standard tuning; open D provides the same relationships at lower absolute pitch.

Focus:

- Straight-bar major chords
- Partial voicings rather than indiscriminate six-string strums
- Voice leading between I, IV, and V areas
- Controlling open-string resonance

## Practice session shapes

### 15-minute diagnostic

- 3 min reference-note matching
- 4 min muting/string crossing
- 4 min movement plus delayed vibrato
- 4 min recorded phrase over a drone or slow blues

### 30-minute development

- 5 min dry pitch and muting check
- 8 min current phase drill
- 7 min short melody
- 8 min backing-track context
- 2 min evidence note: largest audible defect and next target

### 45-minute musical session

- 5 min diagnostic
- 10 min current technical constraint
- 10 min melody or song passage
- 15 min two uninterrupted backing-track takes
- 5 min review and maintenance update

Stop or simplify when pressure, wrist, thumb, or shoulder tension makes pitch correction slower or less controlled.

## Backing-track scenarios

### 1. Slow blues-rock harmony

Asset: `slide-slow-blues-a-60`

Use for:

- Pitch against changing dominant harmony
- Sparse chord-tone phrasing
- Complete-chorus endurance
- Recovery after mistakes

Recommended variants: 50 BPM for diagnosis, 60 BPM standard, 72 BPM for shorter reaction time.

### 2. Modal drone and space

Specification: sustained A/E bed, sparse percussion, 4/4 at 54–66 BPM, sections long enough for eight-beat notes and register changes.

Use for:

- Long-note intonation
- Vibrato width and onset
- String-change noise
- Call-and-response without frequent chord changes

This scenario should become a generated asset under the backing-track layer; until then, a simple DAW drone or looper is sufficient.

## Song and passage use cases

Songs are selected as technique use cases, not as the progression itself. Prefer legally sourced notation or short self-transcribed passages.

Useful categories:

- Vocal-like sustained melody with sparse accompaniment
- Blues or roots-rock fills between vocal phrases
- Standard-tuning slide mixed with ordinary fretting
- Open-tuning chord fragments after the optional branch begins
- Melodies where pitch and vibrato remain exposed rather than masked by dense production

For each chosen passage, record:

- Section/time range
- Techniques exercised
- Tuning
- Difficulty or prerequisite
- Licensed tab/notation source when used

## Milestones

### Beginner

- Stable single notes over three strings
- Basic two-hand muting
- Delayed controlled vibrato
- Short identifiable melody

### Intermediate

- Reliable adjacent and non-adjacent string changes
- Harmony-aware phrases through a 12-bar form
- Mixed fretting and slide
- Two different backing contexts

### Musical-context

- Two uninterrupted recordings in different contexts
- Credible pitch, intentional space, and controlled recovery
- Technique supports the part rather than drawing attention to mechanical problems

## Maintenance

- Active development: short regression check every two weeks.
- Stable technique: monthly.
- Before recording or performance: dry pitch/muting check plus one uninterrupted contextual take.

Regression check:

1. Five reference-note matches.
2. Four muted string changes.
3. One delayed-vibrato note.
4. One eight-bar phrase over a backing track.

Regress only the failed dimension to focused work where practical.

## Completion boundary

The documented path is complete when the progression, setup trade-offs, two backing scenarios, use-case selection rules, baseline procedure, and maintenance check exist.

Personal technique completion still requires actual evidence recordings; repository documentation cannot substitute for those takes.
