# Technique: Slide Foundations

- **ID:** `slide-foundations`
- **Branch:** core
- **Status:** `discovered`
- **Musical purpose:** Play sustained, vocal-like single-note phrases with controlled pitch, vibrato, and string noise in standard tuning before branching into open-tuning vocabulary.
- **Prerequisites:** Basic single-note phrasing, comfortable muting with both hands, ability to hear whether a sustained note is sharp or flat.

## Why this technique exists

Slide playing exposes pitch and muting problems immediately. The goal is not simply to move a slide across strings; it is to make the note start cleanly, settle on pitch, sustain deliberately, and leave without uncontrolled noise.

The initial path uses standard tuning because it transfers directly to existing fretboard knowledge and does not require a dedicated guitar. Open tunings remain a later branch for idiomatic chord shapes and resonant voicings.

## Mechanics

- Place the slide directly over the fret rather than behind it.
- Use the slide with minimal pressure: enough to contact the string, not enough to fret it against the fingerboard.
- Keep one or more fretting-hand fingers behind the slide in light contact with the strings.
- Use picking-hand fingers and palm to silence strings that are not intentionally sounding.
- Approach target pitches from a deliberate direction and settle quickly.
- Generate vibrato around the pitch centre rather than only above or below it.
- Keep wrist, thumb, shoulder, and slide finger relaxed enough to make small corrections.

## Common failure modes

| Failure | Audible symptom | Likely cause | Correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stopping behind the fret | Note sounds flat | Treating slide like fretting | Aim directly over the fret wire |
| Excess pressure | Rattle, pitch instability, fretted contact | Pressing toward the fingerboard | Reduce pressure until only the string contacts the slide |
| Weak rear damping | Sympathetic ringing behind the slide | Fingers behind slide lifted | Maintain light contact behind slide |
| Weak picking-hand muting | Adjacent strings ring during movement | Picking hand leaves unused strings open | Assign thumb/fingers to mute neighbouring strings |
| Wide uncontrolled vibrato | Pitch never settles | Arm movement too large or too fast | Start from a stable centre; reduce width |
| Tension during position shifts | Jerky motion and overshoot | Gripping slide or neck | Slow shift and release thumb pressure |
| Attacking before arrival | Scoop sounds accidental | Pick and slide movement unsynchronized | Arrive first, then attack; add intentional scoops later |

## Progression

### Stage 1 — pitch centre

1. Match a fretted reference note, then reproduce it with the slide.
2. Hold the slide note for four beats without vibrato.
3. Repeat on each string in the middle register.

**Exit gate:** eight of ten notes settle recognizably on pitch within one beat, with no dominant adjacent-string noise.

### Stage 2 — muting and clean attacks

1. Play isolated quarter notes on one string.
2. Alternate between two non-adjacent strings while muting the strings between them.
3. Record dry, without delay or heavy reverb.

**Exit gate:** three consecutive repetitions have clean attacks, clean releases, and no sustained unintended string louder than the target note.

### Stage 3 — controlled movement

1. Slide between two target frets over two beats.
2. Approach the destination from below and above.
3. Add delayed vibrato only after the note settles.

**Exit gate:** destination pitch is stable, travel time matches the pulse, and vibrato begins deliberately.

### Stage 4 — single-string melodies

1. Play a four- to eight-bar melody with limited position range.
2. Preserve phrase rhythm before adding decorative scoops.
3. Compare against a sung or fretted version.

**Exit gate:** melody remains identifiable without accompaniment and pitch errors do not obscure phrase shape.

### Stage 5 — musical context

1. Play over a slow major or minor vamp with space between phrases.
2. Use at least two registers and one controlled string change.
3. Record a complete uninterrupted take.

**Exit gate:** technique supports the phrase, pitch remains credible against harmony, and mistakes do not cause uncontrolled noise or derail the take.

## Exercises

### Reference-note matching

- Fret a note normally.
- Let it ring, then reproduce the same pitch with the slide.
- Record ten attempts and note whether each was initially flat, sharp, or centred.

### Muted string crossing

- Choose strings 2 and 4.
- Alternate one note per bar while explicitly muting strings 1, 3, 5, and 6.
- Repeat with strings 1 and 3, then 3 and 5.

### Delayed vibrato

- Hold a centred note for two beats.
- Add narrow vibrato for two beats.
- Stop the vibrato and return to a stable centre before releasing.

### Two-note phrasing cell

- Choose a root and minor third or major third.
- Play call-and-response phrases using only those pitches.
- Vary rhythm, attack, and vibrato rather than adding notes.

## Musical use cases

### Songs

Use short, legally sourced or self-transcribed passages that emphasize:

- sustained vocal-like melody
- sparse fills between vocal phrases
- controlled position shifts
- expressive but pitch-centred vibrato

Song completion is tracked separately from this technique.

### Backing tracks

1. **Slow blues-rock vamp** — 6/8 or slow 4/4, one chord per bar, enough space to hear intonation.
2. **Drone and modal bed** — sustained root/fifth with sparse percussion, designed for long notes and register movement.

Both should have dry or minimally processed practice renders so delay and reverb do not mask pitch or noise.

## Gear references

- **Required:** slide that fits securely enough to move without gripping; ordinary electric or acoustic guitar.
- **Preferred:** clean or edge-of-breakup tone; moderate sustain; tuner or reference instrument available.
- **Optional:** compressor, light overdrive, short delay, reverb, higher action, heavier strings, dedicated open-tuning setup.

The default path must remain usable on a normal setup. Gear changes may improve tolerance but must not substitute for intonation and muting control.

## Quality gates

| Dimension | Gate |
|---|---|
| Timing | Attacks, arrivals, and releases align with the intended pulse; position shifts do not rush unpredictably. |
| Intonation | Target notes settle within one beat and remain acceptably centred against a reference or harmony. |
| Articulation / muting | Target notes start and end clearly; adjacent and behind-the-slide noise does not compete with the phrase. |
| Dynamics | Attack strength and sustain are deliberate; louder notes reflect phrase intent rather than loss of control. |
| Vibrato | Vibrato begins deliberately, oscillates around the pitch centre, and can stop on pitch. |
| Physical tension | Slide pressure remains light; thumb, wrist, shoulder, and slide finger do not lock during shifts. |
| Repeatability | Three consecutive isolated passes meet the gates in two separate sessions. |
| Musical context | Two uninterrupted recordings over different backing contexts remain recognizably in tune and musically phrased. |

## Promotion rules

- `baseline-recorded`: first representative isolated and melody recording completed.
- `developing`: at least one specific failure mode is being targeted with an exercise.
- `reliable-isolation`: stages 1–3 pass in two sessions.
- `reliable-context`: stages 4–5 pass with two different backing contexts.
- `maintained`: two regression checks pass at least two weeks apart.

## Maintenance

- **Cadence:** every two weeks while active; monthly after three consecutive successful checks.
- **Regression check:** one reference-note sequence, one muted string-crossing sequence, and one uninterrupted eight-bar phrase over a slow backing track.
- **Failure conditions:** persistent pitch drift, uncontrolled adjacent-string noise, or tension that prevents repeatable shifts.
- **Regression action:** return to `developing` for the specific failed dimension; do not reset unrelated dimensions.

## Open decisions for the dedicated slide path

This catalog entry establishes the technique model but does not fully close the broader slide progression issue. Remaining decisions include:

- preferred slide finger and material
- whether standard tuning remains the default beyond foundations
- when to add open E or open G
- setup-specific recommendations based on the actual guitar inventory
- representative song passages and final backing-track assets
