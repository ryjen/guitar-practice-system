# Wah Guitar Path

## Goal

Develop wah control as a repeatable musical technique rather than an uncontrolled effect. The path covers fixed-filter tones, synchronized rhythmic movement, phrase shaping, sustained-note sweeps, and practical signal-chain behaviour.

## Baseline recording

Record the same eight-bar phrase twice:

1. Dry, with no wah movement.
2. With wah, using one deliberate movement plan.

Use a clean or mildly driven tone and record the pedal position strategy:

- parked heel, midpoint, or toe
- one sweep per note
- quarter-, eighth-, or sixteenth-note movement
- phrase-level opening and closing

Compare timing, dynamics, tonal spikes, noise, and whether the wah improves the phrase.

## Signal-chain model

Typical starting order:

```text
guitar -> wah -> gain -> modulation -> delay/reverb -> amp/interface
```

### Placement trade-offs

| Placement | Behaviour | Risk | Use case |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before gain | Classic pronounced sweep; gain reacts to filter | Toe position may become harsh or noisy | Default for expressive rock and funk |
| After gain | Smoother level into distortion; more synth-like filter | Can sound exaggerated and less touch-responsive | Deliberate sound-design option |
| Before compressor | Pedal dynamics remain more exposed | Larger level differences | Expressive lead |
| After compressor | More even response | Noise and tonal peaks may be emphasized | Tight rhythmic work |

Start before gain. Change placement only to solve a specific musical or gain-staging problem.

## Gain staging and noise

- Match bypassed and engaged levels as closely as practical.
- Test heel, midpoint, and toe positions at performance gain.
- Reduce upstream gain when toe-down peaks become brittle or unstable.
- Do not hide timing or pedal noise with heavy delay/reverb during diagnosis.
- Check cable and power noise before attributing hiss to technique.
- Treat squeaks, pot noise, and switching transients as gear-layer concerns when they persist independently of foot control.

## Progression

### Phase 1 — position map

Learn three repeatable positions:

- heel: dark, low-mid emphasis
- midpoint: vocal/nasal centre
- toe: bright, cutting peak

Practice:

1. Play the same chord or note in each position.
2. Return to each position without looking.
3. Record a dry phrase and three parked-position versions.

Gate:

- The three positions are audibly distinct.
- Each position can be recalled consistently across three passes.
- Toe position does not create an uncontrolled volume or harshness spike.

### Phase 2 — parked wah

Use the pedal as a fixed filter rather than moving it continuously.

Practice:

- Find one parked position for clean rhythm.
- Find one for driven lead.
- Move the parked position only between phrases or sections.

Gate:

- The selected position improves separation or character without masking note definition.
- Engaging the pedal does not cause a disruptive level jump.

### Phase 3 — quarter-note synchronization

Practice one complete heel-to-toe or toe-to-heel movement per beat.

Drills:

- Muted single-note pulse at 60 BPM.
- One sweep per quarter note.
- Alternate sweep direction every bar.
- Repeat while accenting beats 2 and 4.

Gate:

- Pedal direction changes align with the click for three consecutive bars.
- Picking and foot motion remain independent enough that one does not drag the other off time.

### Phase 4 — eighth-note movement

Use shorter movements between two chosen positions rather than full travel.

Drills:

- Heel-midpoint eighth notes.
- Midpoint-toe eighth notes.
- Two eighth-note movements per beat over muted sixteenth-note picking.
- Keep the guitar rhythm constant while changing only pedal subdivision.

Gate:

- The filter rhythm remains even and repeatable.
- The pedal does not hit physical endpoints noisily.
- Accents are intentional rather than caused by abrupt foot pressure.

### Phase 5 — sixteenth-note rhythmic control

Use compact pedal travel and stable ankle motion.

Drills:

- Two-position sixteenth-note pulse at 50-70 BPM.
- Accent every fourth movement, then every second.
- Hold the pedal parked for selected sixteenths to create syncopation.
- Combine muted chord scratches with short open chord stabs.

Gate:

- Four bars remain aligned to the click without progressive drift.
- Motion remains small and physically relaxed.
- Filter accents support the groove instead of obscuring the guitar rhythm.

### Phase 6 — phrase shaping

Treat wah movement like vowel articulation.

Practice:

- Open the pedal during a sustained note, then close before release.
- Start a phrase dark and end bright.
- Reverse the shape on the next phrase.
- Use one movement across several notes rather than one movement per note.
- Compare immediate versus delayed pedal movement after the pick attack.

Gate:

- The movement follows phrase intent and is repeatable.
- Note attack, sustain, and release remain audible.
- The sweep does not default to the same shape on every phrase.

### Phase 7 — lead and sustained-note sweeps

Practice:

- Slow heel-to-toe sweep over a sustained bend.
- Toe-to-heel sweep during vibrato.
- Park near the resonant peak, then move away before the next attack.
- Combine wah movement with volume control without losing pitch or timing.

Gate:

- Pitch control remains stable while the foot moves.
- Resonant peaks do not cause clipping or painful level spikes.
- Three different sweep shapes are audibly intentional.

### Phase 8 — mixed musical context

Combine parked, rhythmic, and phrase-shaped use in one take.

Requirements:

- One section uses parked wah.
- One section uses subdivision-synchronized movement.
- One lead phrase uses a sustained sweep.
- At least one section leaves the wah bypassed.

Gate:

- The effect changes have arrangement purpose.
- Timing remains stable through engagement, movement, and bypass transitions.
- Wah use does not become continuous by default.

## Practice sessions

### 15-minute diagnostic

- 3 min heel/mid/toe recall
- 4 min parked-position comparison
- 4 min quarter/eighth synchronization
- 4 min recorded phrase and review

### 30-minute development

- 5 min level and noise check
- 8 min current subdivision drill
- 7 min phrase-shaping exercise
- 8 min backing-track take
- 2 min evidence note

### 45-minute musical session

- 5 min signal-chain check
- 10 min timing drill
- 10 min parked and moving-filter contrast
- 15 min two uninterrupted takes
- 5 min review of timing, peaks, and arrangement usefulness

## Backing-track scenarios

### 1. Clean rhythmic groove

Specification:

- 4/4, 88-104 BPM
- drums and bass with stable sixteenth-note subdivision
- sparse keyboard or clavinet stabs
- one- or two-chord vamp
- clear four- or eight-bar loop boundary

Use for:

- quarter-, eighth-, and sixteenth-note pedal synchronization
- muted rhythm patterns
- parked versus moving-filter contrast
- accent placement

Planned asset ID: `wah-rhythmic-groove`.

### 2. Driven lead bed

Specification:

- 4/4 or 6/8, 60-84 BPM
- sustained harmonic rhythm
- restrained drums and bass
- space for bends and long notes
- no competing upper-mid lead instrument

Use for:

- phrase-level opening and closing
- sustained-note sweeps
- heel-to-toe and toe-to-heel contrast
- gain-staging and resonant-peak checks

Planned asset ID: `wah-driven-lead-bed`.

## Song and passage use cases

Select legally sourced or short self-transcribed passages that expose one of these behaviours:

- rhythmic wah synchronized to muted guitar
- parked-wah rhythm or lead tone
- long-note vowel shaping
- wah movement across a complete phrase
- deliberate bypass/engage contrast between sections

For each passage, record:

- section/time range
- movement strategy
- subdivision or phrase role
- signal-chain placement
- gain setting
- licensed notation source when used

Song completion remains separate from wah-technique mastery.

## Quality gates

| Dimension | Gate |
|---|---|
| Timing | Pedal movement and picking remain aligned to the intended subdivision for at least four bars. |
| Position control | Heel, midpoint, toe, and chosen parked positions are repeatable without visual correction. |
| Dynamics | Engaging or moving the wah does not create unintended level jumps. |
| Tonal control | Resonant peaks are deliberate and do not become harsh, clipped, or dominant. |
| Noise | Mechanical, switching, cable, and gain noise stay below the musical part or are identified as gear faults. |
| Independence | Foot movement does not destabilize picking, fretting, bend pitch, or body balance. |
| Repeatability | Three isolated passes succeed in two sessions. |
| Musical context | Two uninterrupted recordings use different wah roles without defaulting to continuous movement. |

## Maintenance

- Active development: every two weeks.
- Stable technique: monthly.
- Before recording/performance: level/noise check plus one rhythmic and one sustained-note pass.

Regression check:

1. Recall heel, midpoint, toe, and one parked position.
2. Play two bars each of quarter-, eighth-, and sixteenth-note movement.
3. Shape one sustained phrase.
4. Record one eight-bar backing-track take.

Regress only the failed dimension where practical.

## Completion boundary

The documented path is complete when the progression, signal-chain trade-offs, timing drills, fixed and moving-filter exercises, two backing scenarios, quality gates, and maintenance process exist.

Personal completion still requires dry/wah baseline evidence and successful musical-context recordings.
