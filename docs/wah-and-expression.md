# Wah and Expression

## Framing

Treat wah as **expressive filter movement**, not just a funk effect.

The pedal can shape vowels, accents, tension, release, and call-response phrasing. The goal is to make wah feel intentional inside alternative / post-punk / ambient rock vocabulary.

## Core modes

### Accent mode

Use short pedal movements to emphasize rhythmic hits.

Practice:

- play a two-note riff
- keep picking hand steady
- open the wah only on accents
- move the pedal less than expected

### Slow sweep mode

Use the wah as a moving texture over sustained notes or drones.

Practice:

- hold a note or dyad
- sweep slowly over two bars
- listen for vowel changes
- avoid constant full-range movement

### Half-cocked mode

Hold the wah in a resonant middle position and use it like a fixed filter.

Practice:

- find a nasal / vocal midpoint
- play sparse single-note hooks
- adjust position only between phrases
- compare clean, delay, and fuzz variants

### Call-response mode

Use wah motion to make two phrases sound like different voices.

Practice:

- phrase A: darker tone
- phrase B: brighter tone
- keep notes similar but articulation different

## Session shape

1. Dry riff, no wah
2. Accent wah only
3. Half-cocked variation
4. Slow sweep over sustained version
5. Call-response variation
6. Record the best fragment
7. Generate a backing-track spec from the fragment

## Failure modes

- moving the pedal constantly without phrasing intent
- letting wah replace rhythmic precision
- overusing extreme pedal positions
- stacking too many effects before the phrase works dry

## Prompt seed

```text
Generate a 20-minute expressive wah session for alternative rock guitar. Focus on accent wah, half-cocked tone, slow sweeps, and call-response phrasing. Avoid funk clichés. Include a backing-track structure and one MIDI bassline scaffold.
```
